TummyBook

A gut diary for IBS and other digestive symptoms

Not medical advice

TummyBook records what you enter. It does not diagnose, treat or give medical or diet advice. Talk to your doctor or dietitian before changing your diet or medicines.

TummyBook lets you log meals, bowel movements (Bristol scale), symptoms from 0 to 10 and the meds or supplements you took, see your days and months, and share a PDF with your doctor or dietitian. There is no account to sign in to and no ads. This page explains how it works and how to reach us.

Questions

How do I log something?

On Today, tap Meal, Bowel movement, Symptoms or Meds, or + Note. The date and time fill in for you; Earlier today and Yesterday are one tap away. Tap an entry on the timeline to edit or delete it.

How does “Took my usual meds” work?

In Settings › My meds & supplements, mark the ones you take every day as Usual. Then Took my usual meds on Today logs all of them in one tap.

How do I share my diary with my doctor or dietitian?

Open the Report tab, pick 14d, 30d, 90d, All or a Custom range and tap Share PDF. The PDF has a summary, stool types, symptoms, meds, diet phases and challenges, what came before flare-ups, and every entry day by day. Export CSV (all entries) gives you a spreadsheet of every entry. Under Report details you can add your name, who it is for and a note.

What is “Before flare-ups”?

In the Trends tab, TummyBook counts how often each food you ate was followed by a flare-up within 24 or 48 hours. You choose what counts as a flare-up in Settings › What counts as a flare-up?: any symptom at or above a score you pick (5 to 9), a loose stool (types 6–7), a hard stool (types 1–2), and how far back to look. It needs at least 3 flare-ups in the period and only lists foods eaten 3 or more times. It shows timing in your own log, not cause. Many things affect symptoms. Talk to your doctor or dietitian before removing foods.

How do I log diet phases and reintroduction challenges?

Settings › Diet phases & challenges › Open phases & challenges. Add a phase (elimination, reintroduction, personalization or other) with its dates, and a challenge with the food you are testing, the planned amounts for each day from your dietitian and your own outcome. While a challenge runs, Today shows which day of it you are on. TummyBook has no food lists and no food ratings.

How do I move my diary to a new phone?

On the old phone: Settings › Your data › Save a backup file, and keep the file somewhere you can reach from the new phone (email, Files…). On the new phone: Restore from a backup file (on the first screen, or in Settings › Your data). On iPhone, your iPhone’s own backup (iCloud or your computer) may include it. On Android, Google’s phone backup does not include it — use “Save a backup file” before you change phones.

Where is my data? Can I delete it?

Your diary lives on your phone; there is no TummyBook account or cloud. It leaves the phone only when you export it (PDF, CSV or backup file) and send or save it yourself — and, on iPhone, in your phone’s own backup. Settings › Your data › Delete all my data erases your entries, meds, phases, challenges and settings on the phone; it keeps only your purchase status and free-trial dates, if any. I bought the unlock and got a new phone. How do I get it back?

Sign in to the same Apple Account (or Google account on Android), open Settings › Your plan and tap Restore purchase. You will not be charged twice.
